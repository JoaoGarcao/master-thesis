\chapter{Conclusion and Future Work}
\label{cha:conclusion}

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Conflict-Free Replicated Data Types promise convergence without coordination, but the two established ways of verifying
that promise make different trade-offs. Automated tools built on SMT solvers, VeriFx among them, prove algebraic properties
quickly and with little manual effort, but struggle precisely where a CRDT's correctness depends on recursive definitions
or unbounded structures. Deductive platforms such as Why3 handle exactly this kind of reasoning, but ask the programmer
to write and maintain the proof by hand, in a language with its own syntax and its own demands. Neither approach, used
alone, covers what the other is good at.

This dissertation addressed that gap with a single specification language, compiled automatically to both VeriFx and Why3
from the same source. The language hides the syntactic differences between the targets behind a set of constructs,
interfaces, axioms, and directives that let a specification diverge between backends, and
the compiler derives an implementation for each, an auxiliary\/main module pair or a set-based predicate for
Why3, a class extending a shared proof trait for VeriFx, from the same AST.

The evaluation, across ten CRDTs of increasing complexity, showed this design working as intended. For every CRDT, the
specification is shorter than the VeriFx and WhyML code generated from it combined, since a single source replaces two
formalizations. In Why3, every one of the ten case studies satisfies its interface obligations, with Z3 or Alt-Ergo closing every
obligation on its own except one. That exception is \texttt{PNCounter\_CRDT}'s \texttt{compare\_correct}, where a single
manual assertion split a goal the solver could not reach into two goals it closed immediately. VeriFx proves nine of the
same ten CRDTs fully automatically, including \texttt{PNCounter\_CRDT}, and without the auxiliary lemmas that
\texttt{NestedMap} and \texttt{UWMap} need in Why3. It times out on the tenth, \texttt{RGA}, whose correctness depends on
reasoning about an unbounded recursive structure. For \texttt{RGA}, Why3 goes further. With lemmas proved by induction, it
shows that every pair of operations commutes on the element sets, and that every pair except two insertions commutes on
the full state. The two backends therefore complement each other. Automation keeps the effort low for most CRDTs, and
deduction reaches further where automation stops, which is why this dissertation paired the two approaches instead of
choosing between them.

\section{Future Work}
\label{sec:future-work}

The most immediate direction is to complete the Why3 proof of \texttt{RGA} for two insertions, for this pair of operations
Why3 already proves that the element sets converge, but not yet that the elements end up in the same order. This proof
could not be concluded within the time available for this dissertation.

A related idea follows from how the two backends currently work. They prove every property independently, even when one
backend has already established part of what the other is asked to prove. \texttt{RGA} illustrates this, since Why3 proves
that every pair of operations except two insertions commutes, while VeriFx has to establish \texttt{is\_a\_CmRDT} on its
own, including the pairs Why3 has already proved, and times out. Rather than treating the two proofs as fully separate,
the compiler could let a property proved in one backend become an assumption the other is allowed to rely on. VeriFx's
\texttt{is\_a\_CmRDT} obligation could then lean on the cases Why3 has already proved, and symmetrically for a property
only VeriFx manages to close automatically. This would only be sound if the translation between the two representations
preserves exactly the property being trusted, a stronger guarantee than compiling both from the same specification
currently provides.
